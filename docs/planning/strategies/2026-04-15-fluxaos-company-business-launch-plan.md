## Executive Summary

fluxaOS should **not** be marketed as a finished product yet. The right move is a **pre-launch company strategy**:

1. **Position the category clearly** — fluxaOS is an OS for AI workflows.
2. **Build market credibility now** — content, waitlist, founder-led demos, architecture narrative.
3. **Avoid scaling paid acquisition too early** — paid ads before product readiness are usually an expensive way to buy confused clicks.
4. **Prepare legal and financial infrastructure early** so the company can sell as soon as product maturity justifies it.
5. **Use the next 60-90 days to align product readiness, message, and demand generation.**

The company goal right now is not “maximize signups.” It is:
- sharpen positioning,
- validate the ideal customer profile,
- capture serious interest,
- and prepare for an alpha launch without overpromising.

## Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

### Primary ICP
**Technical team leads / engineering managers / startup CTOs** running or experimenting with AI-assisted software workflows who want:
- more control than black-box agent products,
- self-hosted or infrastructure-aware deployment,
- provider-agnostic execution,
- observability, gates, and workflow discipline.

### Secondary ICP
- AI-native dev agencies
- internal platform teams
- applied AI consultancies
- regulated or security-conscious engineering orgs that dislike cloud-only agent products

### Non-ICP for now
Do **not** market broadly to:
- non-technical SMB owners
- generic marketers
- solo no-code users
- teams looking for instant plug-and-play consumer AI

That traffic will be cheap to attract and useless to convert.

## Business Plan Structure

### 1. Problem
Teams experimenting with AI coding and workflow automation lack:
- reliable orchestration,
- governance,
- observability,
- and provider portability.

### 2. Solution
fluxaOS provides a structured operating layer for AI workflows with configurable pipelines, routing, gates, and visibility.

### 3. Market
The market sits at the intersection of:
- AI development tooling,
- workflow orchestration,
- internal developer platforms,
- and self-hosted enterprise AI infrastructure.

### 4. Revenue model (recommended initial posture)
Do **not** overcomplicate monetization yet.

Use a staged model:
- **Now:** waitlist, demos, discovery calls, design-partner conversations
- **Alpha:** founder-led pilot pricing or free design-partner agreements
- **Later:** usage or seat-based commercial plans, likely with self-hosted plus premium capabilities

### 5. Early offers
Near-term offers should be:
- private alpha access,
- design partner program,
- pilot deployment assistance,
- architecture/demo sessions,
- and eventually paid implementation/onboarding.

This is much more realistic than pretending there is a polished self-serve SaaS motion today.

## Facebook Paid Ad Campaign

### Recommendation
**Do not make Facebook a major early spend channel.**

Why:
- B2B technical infrastructure products generally perform poorly on broad Facebook traffic.
- The targeting is weaker for this kind of niche technical buyer than search or direct outbound.
- If the offer is still pre-launch, you will mostly pay to attract curiosity instead of qualified intent.

### If Facebook is used at all
Use it only for:
- remarketing visitors who already hit `fluxaos.io`
- retargeting video viewers from founder content
- very small-budget experiments to validate message angles

### Campaign objective
- Demo request
- Waitlist signup
- Technical founder call

### Creative angles
1. “Your AI agent needs supervision, not vibes.”
2. “An OS for AI workflows — configure, orchestrate, observe.”
3. “Provider-agnostic AI pipelines with gates and real-time visibility.”

### Budget recommendation
- Testing only: **$10-$25/day max**
- Retargeting first, cold traffic second
- Kill fast if lead quality is trash

### Success metric
Not CTR. Not impressions.  
Success = qualified technical conversations.

## Legal Plan

### Immediate legal priorities
1. **Form the company properly** (likely LLC or equivalent, jurisdiction-dependent)
2. **Define founder ownership and IP assignment clearly**
3. **Choose and document the software license strategy**
4. **Prepare Terms of Use and Privacy Policy for the public site**
5. **Create alpha / pilot agreement templates**
6. **Review trademark availability for `fluxaOS`**

### License issue
The repo strategy docs already flag a serious risk around AGPL for commercial adoption. This needs explicit resolution.

Recommended action:
- get actual legal review on AGPL vs BSL vs dual-license path,
- because enterprise buyers can get skittish fast,
- and this choice affects distribution, trust, and sales motion.

### Legal docs needed before external selling
- Terms of Service
- Privacy Policy
- Cookie notice if tracking/remarketing is used
- Alpha disclaimer / early access terms
- Pilot or design-partner agreement
- Contractor/employee IP assignment agreements

### Risk note
If you run ads and collect customer data without your legal basics in place, that is sloppy and unnecessary. Fix the boring stuff early.

## Financial Plan

### Near-term financial posture
Keep it lean and ugly in a good way.

Primary goals:
- control burn,
- track tooling/infrastructure spend,
- separate company finances immediately,
- and define what a qualified pilot would need to be worth.

### Financial setup checklist
1. Business bank account
2. Bookkeeping system
3. Monthly P&L tracking
4. Budget owner and approval rules
5. Tooling and infra cost tracking
6. Ad spend caps and review cadence
7. Revenue assumptions for pilot / alpha pricing

### Suggested budgeting posture (pre-launch)
- **Product/dev:** highest priority
- **Brand/site/content:** second priority
- **Paid ads:** small experiments only
- **Legal/accounting:** non-negotiable setup cost
- **Nice-to-have marketing fluff:** cut it

### Metrics to track now
- monthly infra/tool spend
- website traffic
- waitlist conversions
- demo requests
- design-partner conversations
- cost per qualified lead
- founder time per lead / pilot

### Financial truth
Until there is proof of repeatable customer demand, assume this is a validation business, not a scale business.
